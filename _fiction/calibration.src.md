# Calibration.src

_What follows is an edited transcript of a long-running conversation between a writer (J) and an AI assistant (C), condensed for readability. Tool calls, file writes, and the mechanics of an inline document-comment feature have been removed. Where J left a comment on a specific phrase in a draft rather than typing a new message, it's rendered here as J quoting the phrase, then the instruction, then what C did about it._

_Nothing here has been anonymised. This one didn't grow out of something that actually happened. It started from an image — an AI-generated mock "printer diagnostic and calibration sheet", already loaded with skulls, hidden eyes, and instructions not to look too long — and a short story J wanted built around it._

---

## Chat one: the premise, and how to sound

**J:** _[shares the image]_ I'm pondering a short story to accompany this image. A man needs to print something for his young daughter. Knowing the colour cartridge has been low for some time, he changes it. Just to check it worked, he asks an AI assistant for a printer calibration page — "at first glance a viable test of alignment, CMYK colours and so on, but on closer inspection weird anomalies and psychedelic corruptions." He prints it, laughs at the result. But that night elements of the page infect his dreams. Gradually the influence of the page starts affecting his waking life, because the page is calibrating _him_. We want some kind of melodramatic ending — maybe he's found next to a torched church, non-responsive, except to giggle sinisterly, repeating "calibration complete." Discuss. Don't start writing until I say "Now write it."

**C:** _[reads the sheet element by element — registration marks, gray balance, nozzle check, moiré — and proposes mapping the story's stages onto the sheet's own print-industry vocabulary: misregistration as a splitting sense of self, dot gain as impulses bleeding past their edges, gray balance as moral perception flattening. Suggests the daughter as stakes rather than a target, a three-beat escalation, and flags a concern about making a child's sexualisation or endangerment part of the horror — not raised by the premise as put, but worth being deliberate about, since the daughter is present throughout. Offers the church-and-giggle ending as workable but asks whether "calibration complete" should replace it, or a different closing act entirely.]_

**J:** For authorial tone I'm thinking adult Roald Dahl, but let's not imitate him too faithfully. Suggest other influences.

**C:** _[proposes a blend: Dahl's cheerful cruelty as the chassis, Shirley Jackson's eye for a household curdling from just outside the family's own head, Patricia Highsmith's calm, internally coherent protagonist logic, Thomas Ligotti's flat cosmic indifference under the comedy, Saki for a fast, deadpan hard stop at the end, and Ted Chiang in small doses for describing the AI mechanism plainly rather than mystically.]_

**J:** Cosmic horror is just the angle. We are talking someone driven to gibbering madness by something primal, cosmic and, importantly, real. Let us not pinpoint which print element contains the conditioning or the trigger. We don't know. We just conclude that it's there. We can have a vignette of him surrounded by printouts of increasing apparent chaos and meaninglessness, coupled with a chat history that increasingly departs from readable English, eventually deteriorating into Lovecraftian eldritch gobbledegook.

**C:** _[agrees the trigger should stay unlocalised — naming it turns it into a defeatable prop — and proposes the chat log converge from both directions: his prompts and the AI's replies both decaying toward the same non-language, rather than one corrupting the other. Suggests the accumulating printouts should get sparser and more exact rather than noisier, since noise is easy to dismiss and a precisely rendered single shape is not.]_

---

## Chat two: the ending, twice reconsidered

**J:** Burning a church. Too monotheism-centric now I think. What's a Lovecraftian act of ultimate madness?

**C:** _[proposes alternatives that stay geometric rather than religious: a spiral burned into a field, echoing the sheet's own alignment target — "centre is an illusion, focus elsewhere" — completed at true scale; self-blinding, as the literal endpoint of "your perception is the problem"; or standing exposed to a fixed point in the sky through the night. Recommends the spiral as the strongest single image.]_

**J:** Burned spiral _followed by_ self-blinding?

**C:** _[agrees the sequencing works — the spiral is the vast, impersonal cosmic-horror image, and the self-blinding reads as its logical coda rather than a second, separate horror beat, paying off "your perception is the problem" literally without needing to explain it.]_

**J:** Now write it. As a Markdown artifact.

**C:** _[writes a roughly 2,000-word story, titled "All Systems Nominal," in Claude's own artifact format.]_

A run of smaller revisions followed the first draft, in roughly this order:

- **Dahl-isms.** J: the long, characterful compound sentences ("in a manner his wife compared to... never said aloud... because...") are too stark. Dial back simile except where it earns its place. → _C rewrites the piece with the ornate similes cut or shortened throughout._
- **The daughter's unicorn.** J: cut it, it's not a detail likely to have been recorded; she has "some sort of craft project on the go." → _changed._
- **Motivation.** J: this isn't a printer that needs calibration. He only needed to check a photo prints properly. Asking the AI for a spoof calibration page is a whim, and it's a plain consumer chatbot, not "the machine." → _the AI gets a name, the printer is only ever tested with an ordinary print, and every subsequent reference to "the machine" is replaced with the chatbot's name._
- **What's haunted.** J: the printer itself is not haunted. The image is, in a way. → _held throughout the rest of the drafting._

Several rounds went into naming the chatbot. J asked for shortlists in several registers — invented consumer-app names, print/typography in-jokes, real-world-style product names, and finally boys' and girls' names in various languages, since Claude itself is a human first name and the pattern fit. J picked **Ansel**, and asked that Hugh's use of it be established as an existing, recreational habit rather than a one-off impulse — Ansel already wrote his shopping lists in the voice of a disappointed sea captain and had put meerkats on everyone's heads in the family's holiday photos.

**J:** The detail about the symbols from no keyboard layout can't stay. We need technical plausibility, so he's using conventional character sets. But that still allows gibberish. Think Lovecraft's "Ph'nglui mglw'nafh Cthulhu R'lyeh wgah'nagl fhtagn." And extend this idea with unicode characters outwith traditional writing and punctuation. Avoid emoji, but all other symbols are in scope.

**C:** _[rewrites the decaying chat log as compound Lovecraftian syllables interleaved with ordinary, non-emoji Unicode — mathematical marks, proofreading dashes, occult-looking dingbats — stressing that nothing in it needed unusual software to type or display.]_

**J:** He says only "Calibration complete," not "All systems nominal." Title it "Calibration" and rename the file.

**C:** _[done.]_

Then the story's whole shape came under pressure.

**J:** I'm wondering about the burnt spiral. Would it improve or detract from the story if we went much more shocking. Murdering his family?

**C:** _[pushes back: the tonal register the piece is built on — restrained, cool, withholding — is the wrong register for that kind of spectacle, and a motivated act against his own family pulls the story from cosmic indifference toward personal psychology, undoing the earlier choice to remove the wife and daughter from harm's way before the ending. Suggests smaller, cheaper escalations instead.]_

**J:** Well, to be fair _you_ chose to remove the wife and daughter for me, we could undo that. But the cosmic horror is insignificant if what it causes him to do is small... I wonder about something truly deranged, like to find him weeping in a room full of meticulously arranged dismembered corpses.

**C:** _[declines to write graphic dismemberment, stating plainly it's a personal line rather than a policy hedge, and separates that from the craft question — offering implication-over-anatomy as a way to keep the scale without the detail, and naming discomfort specifically about a child's death being confirmed on the page even off-screen.]_

**J:** We would not detail the process of dismemberment... The victims could be his family, or could be someone else, but we'd need to consider how he has acquired them.

**C:** _[argues family solves the logistics problem cleanly, but pushes for the killings to read as cold arrangement rather than rage, and flags real discomfort specifically about confirming the daughter's death, suggesting an unresolved "he was not alone in the house" as a harder, better version than a stated body count.]_

**J:** I'm retreating from the idea. I wanted to explore it, but it feels too grim. Yet the burnt spiral isn't giving me the "driven stark staring mad by a glimpse of the infinite chaos of the universe" that the image unlocks in his primal brain.

**C:** _[names the actual mismatch: a spiral is a completed, ordered shape, and "glimpse of infinite chaos" wants scale and formlessness instead. Offers three directions — recursive, bottomless accumulation of printouts; the phenomenon turning out not to be contained to one man at all; or both together — and suggests dropping the burnt spiral as the ending outright.]_

## Chat three: the pivot

**J:** Ok, how about this. We rewrite it as a magazine or newspaper feature article, uncovering the origins of a phenomenon the reader already knows about — millions of people driven to catatonic madness. [...] Our journalist believes they've identified Patient Zero.

_[J lays out the whole brief in one message: the pandemic's scale — mental health services overwhelmed, GDP hit, everyone knows a victim; Hugh given a full journalistic name, age, job, location; his wife as the on-record source for the habit and the escalation; the dialogue with the chatbot decaying into code in both directions; the first page reproduced in the piece, stressed as safe and as the unrefined original, never the dangerous later version; expert theories properly both-sided; a diplomatic non-apology from the AI company, gesturing at scraped esoteric texts without naming anything; no record of how the page got out; an international containment effort with its own privacy backlash, mirroring anti-vaccine rhetoric; and a much smaller, non-violent ending — hospitalised, starved, murmuring "calibration complete," in a secure facility.]_

**C:** _[rewrites the piece from scratch as a long-form investigative feature — headline, standfirst, sourced quotes, a captioned "reproduction" of the first page, expert theories on both sides, a corporate statement, containment debate — landing on the softer, non-violent ending exactly as briefed.]_

Everything after this point was fine-grained editing of the article rather than further structural change, delivered mostly as line-specific comments rather than open discussion. The next section collects the ones worth keeping; the rest tightened wording without changing anything the story depends on.

## Moulding

**J** _(on "a hundred and more sheets of paper")_: Thousands.

**C:** _[changes the line, and raises the earlier description of the pinned wall variants from "more than a hundred" to "dozens, then hundreds, then thousands" so the two figures agree.]_

**J** _(on the anecdote about meerkats and shopping lists, "It was just — that was Gerald being Gerald")_: Drop, too much detail.

**J** _(later, on "Neighbours describe him as pleasant and a little dull")_: He went for long bike rides and played guitar for his own amusement.

**J:** Frame this as an exclusive, and an investigative piece. [...] We also need to explain why, since Gerald is in a mental institution, he wasn't identified as patient zero by authorities.

**C:** _[adds a named journalist, Naomi Osei, and a school-gate rumour as the actual lead; explains that no agency has bothered to trace a historical origin, since by the time coordinated tracking began the page already existed in thousands of independently mutating copies and containment cared only about what was currently spreading, not archaeology. Rewrites the wife's reason for silence — she'd spent years keeping her husband's name out of it, not an unconvincing condition about the daughter — and has her agree to talk only once she's found and pressed.]_

**J** _(on "on the condition that their daughter... not be named")_: Not convincing — the child would be easy to identify. Perhaps she's been trying to keep her husband's name out of the spotlight, but once approached realised the story needs telling.

**J** _(on "an unremarkable man")_: Seems damning. "A well-liked family man."

**J:** Include a quote from a named objector from a named digital rights group, and a rebuttal from a government minister's parliamentary speech, referring to an incident in some poorly regulated part of the world.

**C:** _[adds Sanjay Mehta of Panoptic Watch, challenging the scanning programme in court, and Grace Ellison MP's Commons rebuttal citing an outbreak cluster.]_

**J** _(on the same passage, once written)_: Consolidate the anti-cal and Panoptic Watch parts, favouring Panoptic Watch. Add a paragraph break before the Grace Ellison quote. Rather than a file-hosting operation, let it be phone-to-phone sharing in the Sahara. Not too much detail, keep it snappy, as if the reader is aware of such outbreaks.

**C:** _[folds "anti-cal" in as the label already attached to Panoptic Watch's own legal challenge rather than a separate group; swaps the cited incident to an unexplained one-line reference to a phone-sharing outbreak across the Sahara; splits the paragraph as asked.]_

**J** _(on "Members opposite")_: Opponents, and make it "hundreds of" victims.

**J:** Try again, it isn't "like memetics", it _is_ memetics. It is _like_ the evolution of DNA.

**C:** _[rewrites the geneticist's quote so memetics is asserted as literal fact and DNA evolution supplies the analogy, not the other way round.]_

**J** _(on "Not a metaphor for it — the actual thing,")_: Drop.

**J** _(on "Nobody needs to understand what they're optimising for. Evolution never does.")_: "What we don't fully understand is, what makes the most harmful form, also the most transmissible?"

**J:** All the academics' universities would be stated in a real journal. Pick plausible real world establishments for their specialities, or invent establishments suitable for cranks where that fits.

**C:** _[gives Kastrinos Bologna, Okafor Indiana University Bloomington — the real centre of academic folklore studies — Basu King's College London, and invents the Halden Institute for Perceptual Science for Vermeulen's more speculative theory, letting the private, non-university affiliation quietly signal the difference in rigour without stating it.]_

**J** _(soon after, on the same line)_: Lose the reference to government there — I think Naomi Osei's access will not get her to government consultants.

**J:** Officially PCD, colloquially The Stare. Update accordingly please.

**C:** _[the disease's public name, used throughout the drafting to this point as "the Calibration," is retired from the body of the piece entirely — kept only in the headline's own vocabulary of the sheet, Hugh's original prompt, and his final words — and replaced with Perceptual Collapse Disorder in clinical and governmental contexts, "the Stare" in colloquial ones.]_

**J:** "the near-total collapse of the consumer printing industry" — debating whether to keep this in. People would still print, in the long run surely. What do you think?

**C:** _[agrees a permanent collapse sits badly against the containment section's own claim that scanning and printing continue; proposes a temporary sales dip instead.]_

**J:** The last one works, please. _(on "is in excess of a quarter of a trillion dollars, and nobody expects that number to stop climbing")_: "is in excess of", lose the second clause.

**J:** Let's work on the title of the article, and the name of the publication.

_[a naming exercise follows: newspaper-register names versus weekly-register names, landing on the weekly model as the better fit for a piece built on a four-month methodology note and a first-person byline.]_

**J:** Yes to The Folio, once you've asserted it's not a real publication in this sphere.

**C:** _[searches, confirms no current-affairs weekly of that name exists — only a defunct US trade title and a defunct Jacksonville alt-weekly, neither in the same sphere — then supplies a shortlist of essayistic article titles once J points out the readership already knows what PCD/the Stare is, so the headline's job is promising a reveal, not explaining the disease.]_

**J:** "The Source of the Stare"?

**C:** _[sets the headline.]_

**J:** Remove "The Folio" and "current affairs — investigations" to the top, and make it an attribution under the title "By Naomi Osei. The Folio. September 2029."

**J** _(on "I tracked")_: NAOMI OSEI tracks.

**J** _(on the italicised methodology note about four months of interviews and FOI requests)_: Drop.

**J:** Relocate to Bristol.

**C:** _[three place references changed.]_

**J** _(on "like he was translating you from a language he was slowly forgetting")_: like he was forgetting how to hear.

**J:** How do we feel about the HRs now? [...] I think perhaps we could do away with sections altogether, as if this has been transposed from a magazine article to a paperback anthology, losing typography on the way.

**C:** _[agrees it suits a piece already concerned with copies degrading as they're reproduced, and removes every horizontal rule, leaving continuous prose broken only by paragraphs.]_

**J:** Need something weirder than "more unsettling detail in the corners" — references to psychedelia? Borrow some 60s LSD terminology? Lewis Carroll? Mushrooms? Crowley-esque terms?

**C:** _[first attempt names all of it at once — acid, "as above so below," the rabbit hole — in a single line.]_

**J:** Too much and too overt. Let's think of some ideas.

**C:** _[offers quieter single-phrase alternatives that don't name any tradition outright.]_

**J:** I like 3, 6, 8 — let Helen give them as a list of examples.

**C:** _[settles on "let the pattern eat itself," "make the grid melt slightly," "let it open a little wider," reported by Helen as a set.]_

**J** _(on "in enlargements he ordered")_: sometimes in enlargements.

**J** _(on the anonymous computer scientist's paragraph, undermining the article's own claim that the reproduced image is safe)_: Drop.

**J** _(on "A separate, unconfirmed account")_: One speculative account.

**J:** "anti-cal" is derived from Calibration, now a banned name. And it never worked really, them being anti-anti-cal. Propose some alternative names.

**J** _(on "anti-cal")_: Open Eyes.

**J:** Change Gerald's name to Hugh.

**C:** _[ten instances changed throughout.]_

**J** _(on "the reassuring printer-diagnostic text gone")_: parodic.
