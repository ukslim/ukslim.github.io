**C:** **How big is a pipe?** A pipe was half a tun, or 1008 pints, which is about 126 US gallons, roughly 477 litres. A Madeira pipe probably ran a little smaller in practice, so call it 400 to 480 litres.

Eight pipes come to about 3,500 to 3,800 litres. Split among 37 men, that's about 100 litres each. Even a single pipe is 11 to 13 litres a man, or around fifteen bottles. A one-night binge can't drain eight pipes, and it probably couldn't drain one. Option B doesn't work as I pitched it.

**Rules on drunkenness.** The articles mostly controlled where and when men drank, not how much. Roberts's articles gave every man equal title to the fresh provisions, or strong liquors, at any time seized, to use at pleasure unless a scarcity makes it necessary to vote a retrenchment. Our Article 4 is based on that. I also remember two details I haven't been able to confirm:

- Roberts's lights-out clause sent anyone still drinking after eight up onto the open deck, away from naked flames below.
- Low's articles punished a man found drunk during an engagement.

As far as I know, no surviving set bans drinking outright. Their concern was fire and fighting. The tradition is also that Roberts's crew were still drunk from a recent prize when the navy caught them in 1722. That's often repeated, but I haven't checked it.
