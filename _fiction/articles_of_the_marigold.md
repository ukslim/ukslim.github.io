**ARTICLES** of the _Marigold_. Agreed and sworn at sea, 9 January 1697.

**1.** Every man has a vote on big decisions.

**2.** No man is taken against his will.

**3.** Food and drink are shared. A vote can ration them if we run short.

**4.** Shares are equal. The captain and quartermaster get a little more.

**5.** A man who loses a limb is paid from the common stock first.

**6.** Lights out at eight. No gambling for money.

---

### Article 1: Every man has a vote on big decisions

Ansell counts hands. It's the part of the job he likes best.

Thirty-six men have a vote. Most sit on the main hatch. A few hang in the rigging and say they're only listening. Alongside, held fast with grapnels, a Portuguese ship lies with her colours down. Her crew sit in a row on her deck. Ten of the _Marigold_'s men stand over them with pistols and shout their votes across the gap.

The question is what to do with her.

On a navy ship, one man would decide. On the _Marigold_, everyone does. It takes all afternoon. Captain Dunning stands at the back. A captain can be voted out by Thursday. He lets the others speak first. Ansell has been quartermaster for ten weeks and still isn't used to being asked. He holds the book and waits.

Peter Voss wants to burn her. "The last ship we let go told everyone where we were heading." Someone else wants to keep her. When it comes to Dunning, he says strip her and let her go. Then they argue for a long time about what "strip" means.

Nobody has mentioned the wine yet. Eight pipes in her hold, marked for Barbados. Nobody will buy it from the _Marigold_ this side of the Cape. By the time anyone might, Voss says, it will be vinegar. He's probably right. It isn't treasure. It's a lot of drink.

Ansell has no opinion about wine. It worries him that his is the one that gets written down.

They vote to strip her and let her go. It isn't close. The wine goes into the _Marigold_'s hold with everything else. Nobody can think of a reason not to.

Before they cast off the grapnels, they ask the Portuguese if any of them want to sign. Two do. Ansell notes that they asked, which the navy never did.

### Article 2: No man is taken against his will

They take the cooper too.

One of the men in the row keeps looking at the hatch. When the boarders came over the side, he looked at the casks before he looked at the guns. He's a cooper from Funchal, hired by the wine's owner to see eight pipes safe to Barbados. Dunning notices. He moves that they keep him. A ship with eight pipes aboard needs a man who knows pipes.

Ansell reads out Article 2. He does it for the look of the thing. Half the crew helped write it, and most of them were taken once themselves. It's the only paper any of them has ever had a hand in. The hatch goes quiet. Then Voss says it was meant for sailors, and nobody asks who decided a cooper isn't one. They vote. The cooper stays. The book says _for the good of all_.

His name is Tomé Lobo. He's about fifty, and he has enough English to get by. He asks for two things. He wants to stow the pipes himself, and he wants a lamp. Ansell can give him the first. The lamp needs a vote of its own, because of Article 6.

That night Ansell finds him in the hold, with the lamp. All eight pipes are wedged hard against the galley bulkhead, six feet from the cook's fire.

Ansell has brought the page with the articles. He isn't sure why. It seems rude not to offer. "You could sign. You'd have a vote."

Lobo looks at it for a long time. He can't read much English. Then he asks what his name on it would say if the ship were taken.

Ansell opens his mouth. He hasn't thought of that. It's the first thing any lawyer would think of.

"I thought so," Lobo says. "Mind the lamp."

Ansell goes back up. It happened to him at nineteen. A collier in the Downs. A man with a shilling. He signed the muster book that night. It was put in front of him. He has seen the page since. It's a good signature. Anyone reading it would think a young man wanted to go to sea.

### Article 3: Food and drink are shared

Voss wants to know what they've got. They vote to open one pipe and find out.

Lobo draws it on deck, with a copper tube as long as his arm. He calls it a thief. He pushes it through the bung, closes his thumb over the top and lifts it out. The wine in it is pale, like weak cider.

The quartermaster tastes first. Ansell drinks. It's thin and sharp, and it catches at the back of his throat. He used to have no opinion about wine. Now he has one, and it isn't good.

The cup goes round. Voss drinks it off and makes a face. "Ship's wine."

He moves that it be issued as the ration, half a pint a man a day, before it turns.

Dunning asks Lobo if it will keep.

Lobo thinks about it. "It'll change."

Nobody asks how. He can speak. He can't vote. Everybody knows it and nobody says so.

The motion passes. Ansell works it out on the edge of the page. Eight pipes, thirty-seven men, half a pint a day. It will last them most of a year.

### Article 4: Shares are equal

The Cape. Madagascar. The mouth of the Red Sea. Ansell keeps the book through all of it.

The _Marigold_ takes four ships in a year. Two are poor. One fights. When she does, nobody votes. Dunning gives the orders and the men do as they're told. It's the one hour when the _Marigold_ is run like a King's ship, and they all agreed to that too. Afterwards he goes back to standing at the back. Three of theirs die and one of ours, and Ansell writes all four names in the book. Article 5 pays for a lost limb and says nothing about a lost man. They vote to treat it as if it did.

The last is a heavy ship out of Surat, low with cloth and pepper. She makes every man aboard rich enough to be careful.

Ansell casts the shares on the deck with a ruler and a lot of argument. Every man gets the same. Dunning gets a little more. So does Ansell. Everyone has agreed to that.

Lobo gets nothing. Nobody forgets it exactly. It just isn't written anywhere.

He rehoops the pipes in the Madagascar heat. He mends the water casks. He sleeps on a pallet beside the wine with his back to the planking. Men start leaving tobacco for him. Voss doesn't.

Every day each man has his half pint. Every day it's a little different from the day before, so nobody notices. After Surat the whole ship smells of pepper, the bread and the hammocks and the men. Kit, who is fifteen, says the wine tastes of pepper now. Everyone agrees with him.

Every six weeks or so a pipe runs dry. They haul it up on deck, give it three cheers and roll it over the side. The youngest hand gets to knock out the bung first, so Kit does it twice. Lobo watches each one go. He says nothing.

### Article 6: Lights out at eight

They sell at St Mary's, off Madagascar. Ships come all the way from New York to wait there for crews like theirs.

The New York man comes aboard after dark, in a good coat. He wants to see the hold before he makes an offer. That means a lamp, and the lamp means Article 6. They vote to suspend it for an hour. It's the quickest vote of the voyage.

He walks the hold with Ansell. He looks at the cloth and the pepper and the chests. Then he stops at the galley bulkhead, where one pipe is left, half full. He asks what it is.

Lobo draws it. The wine in the thief is the colour of strong tea.

The smell reaches them before the cup reaches the New York man. Orange peel. Dried fruit. Clove. Something darker underneath. It's warm and sharp and sweet all at once, and nothing else on the ship smells like it. It doesn't smell of pepper.

The New York man drinks. Then he asks where it's been.

Ansell tells him. Cape Verde, the Cape, Madagascar, the Red Sea, back across the line. The man nods at each place as if he's adding it up. He asks how much they had. Eight pipes. He asks where the rest is. Ansell points at the sea.

The New York man names a price for the half pipe. Then he names what a full one would have fetched in London, and he says it gently.

Ansell is the quartermaster. He does the sum before he can stop himself. Seven and a half pipes. It comes to more than a man's share for each of them.

The New York man looks at Lobo. "You're from the island."

"Yes."

The man looks at him a while longer. So does Ansell. Lobo knocks the bung back in with the heel of his hand.

They sell him the half pipe. It's the one thing aboard he doesn't haggle over.

### Article 7: Added by vote

The New York man's boat leaves at two in the morning, with the half pipe lashed in the stern. Ansell has the watch.

Lobo comes up the ladder with his mallet and the thief and nothing else. He stops by the rail. Ansell has the book under his arm. Neither of them says anything.

It would take one shout. Thirty-six men would be on deck before Lobo reached the boat.

Ansell thinks of a man with a shilling, and of a page put in front of him at nineteen. He looks at the sea.

Lobo goes down the side. The boat pulls away into the dark.

In the morning the hatch is full before anyone has called a vote. Voss does most of the talking. Dunning lets him. Lobo drank their wine and watched them drink it. He slept beside it every night and let them call it pepper. Nobody disagrees. Nobody asks how a man got off the ship on the quartermaster's watch. Dunning looks at Ansell for a long time. He doesn't ask either.

Ansell writes the new article. It takes him one try.

**7.** A forced man who keeps back what he knows is to be marooned.

He reads it aloud. Voss puts his hand up first.

Ansell counts hands. Thirty-six voted. Thirty-six are aboard.

He writes both numbers down.
